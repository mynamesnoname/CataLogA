# Final Report: TARGETID 39628226921761826

## 1. Overview

TARGETID 39628226921761826 was observed twice, producing two discrepant redshift solutions: **z = 1.4990** (H1, coadd A) and **z = 1.2933** (H2, coadd B), both classified GALAXY, both with **ZWARN = 0**. This gives |Δz| = 0.206 (Δv ≈ 25,700 km/s). DELTACHI2 favors H1 (106) over H2 (41). Since both exposures report clean ZWARN flags, this is a genuine strict catastrophe — at least one redshift is wrong despite a formally "good" fit. At first glance this looks like a case where one exposure carries a strong, clean spectral anchor and the other does not.

## 2. Verdict

- **HS verdict: PREFER_H1** (z = 1.4990)
- **RA verdict: PREFER_H1** (z = 1.4990), confidence HIGH

**HS and RA agree** on which redshift to trust. Both anchor their verdict on the same dominant feature: a resolved, high-significance emission line at 9312–9314 Å in coadd A, identified as [O II] 3727 at z ≈ 1.499, essentially exact to four decimal places. Agreement across independent synthesis (HS) and root-cause (RA) analyses substantially strengthens confidence in this conclusion, though both flag residual caveats (see below).

## 3. What SH Found

- **H1 (z=1.4990, coadd A):** Verdict SUPPORTED. Anchored by [O II] 3727 as an extremely strong CWT detection (ridge=24, SNR=96.4) at 9312.8 Å, offset only −0.9 Å from prediction, implied z=1.4987. Independently corroborated by [Ne V] 3426 (ridge=9, SNR=12.2, implied z=1.4960), suggestive of AGN-like ionization. No broad QSO lines (C IV, He II, C III], Mg II) were detected, but this was attributed to low overall SNR (0.8) rather than contradicting evidence.
- **H2 (z=1.2933, coadd B):** Verdict NOT SUPPORTED (weak evidence). Only [O II] reached LIKELY (CWT SNR=9.3, offset +5 Å, implied z=1.2946), with Hδ MARGINAL but inconsistent (35 Å offset, unconfirmed by independent fit). No Ca K/H absorption pair, no broad lines, and [Ne V] was masked/inconclusive. SH characterized this as "insufficient signal to confirm," not active refutation.

## 4. What FA Confirmed or Rejected

- **H1:** 0 KEEP, 1 FLAG (9313.7 Å [O II]), 1 REMOVE ([Ne V] at 8561.5 Å, judged flat/noise-level, no real peak). The surviving FLAG feature was rated HIGH confidence as real — a smooth ~7-pixel resolved profile peaking at 18.6 against a continuum RMS of ~0.2, vastly exceeding neighboring noise. It was only downgraded from KEEP to FLAG due to mandatory caution in the >7800 Å OH airglow zone, not because of any defect in the line itself.
- **H2:** 0 KEEP, 0 FLAG, 2 REMOVE (both [O II] at 8547.0 Å and Hδ at 9409.0 Å rejected as HIGH-confidence noise/artifacts — single-pixel bumps indistinguishable from, or smaller than, surrounding OH-forest spikes). FA's oii_morphology check found the 8547 Å feature was not even among the top-2 brightest local peaks, failing the prominence test.

The FA audits are decisive: H1 retains one genuinely strong feature, while H2 retains nothing.

## 5. Catastrophe Source from RA

**RA's catastrophe_type: `noise_overfit`**

RA's key diagnostic: the [O II] line is real and astrophysical in coadd A (SNR~96, resolved doublet-consistent profile, exact wavelength match to z=1.4990). Critically, at the *identical* observed wavelength in coadd B, there is no emission at all — instead large **negative** flux excursions (−3.6 to −3.8), consistent with a localized sky over-subtraction/bad-pixel defect that inverted the source's only strong line into an apparent absorption trough. Because OH airglow residuals recur at fixed wavelength in every exposure, the absence of any positive feature in B at 9312 Å actually *proves* A's line is astrophysical rather than a skyline artifact. Deprived of its one true anchor, redrock in exposure B instead fit a shared continuum bump (present in both A and B, but too broad — FWHM 36 Å — to be a real narrow line) plus several single-pixel OH-forest spikes (e.g., a 5.09 spike at 9624.8 Å), arriving at a spurious z=1.2933 with a comparatively shallow minimum (DELTACHI2=41). RA explicitly rules out line confusion (the two hypotheses anchor at different wavelengths, not the same feature mislabeled), template mismatch, and genuine astrophysical variability (an SNR-96 line cannot physically vanish between exposures).

## 6. Bottom Line

Trust **z = 1.4990 (H1, coadd A)** — both HS and RA independently converge on this redshift, anchored by an unambiguous, high-SNR [O II] 3727 detection at 9312.8 Å with FA-confirmed reality. The z=1.2933 solution (H2) is a noise/artifact-driven overfit in coadd B, where a sky-subtraction defect destroyed the target's real emission line, leaving redrock to fit OH residuals and a non-diagnostic continuum bump. RA sets **reobserve = false**, judging coadd A already decisive; HS is somewhat more cautious, noting H1 rests on a single line and recommending a confirming re-observation or near-IR follow-up (to catch Hβ/[O III] beyond 9824 Å) if independent verification is desired.
